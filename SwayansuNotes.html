Blockchain Technology

A distributed ledger that records once and cannot quietly be rewritten — how it works, what it's good for, and where it still struggles.

BLOCK 01 — DEFINITION

Definition and concept

Blockchain is a distributed, decentralized digital ledger technology that records transactions across multiple computers (nodes), designed so that recorded data cannot be altered retroactively without altering every subsequent block and gaining consensus of the network.

It was originally conceptualized as the underlying technology for Bitcoin (Satoshi Nakamoto, 2008), but has since evolved into a general-purpose technology used across industries.

BLOCK 02 — MECHANISM

How it works

  1. Transaction initiation — a user initiates a transaction (e.g. transfer of cryptocurrency, data, or asset ownership).
  2. Block formation — the transaction is grouped with others into a "block."
  3. Validation — nodes validate the transaction using a consensus mechanism (e.g. Proof of Work, Proof of Stake).
  4. Hashing — each block gets a unique cryptographic hash, and stores the previous block's hash too — forming the "chain."
  5. Addition to chain — once validated, the block is added permanently, visible to all participants.
  6. Immutability — altering a block changes its hash, breaking the chain and alerting the network.
BLOCK 03 — TRAITS

Key characteristics

  • Decentralization — no single authority controls the network.
  • Immutability — once recorded, data cannot be easily changed.
  • Transparency — all participants can view the ledger.
  • Consensus-driven — validity is agreed upon by network nodes, not a central party.
  • Cryptographic security — uses hashing and public/private key encryption.

BLOCK 05 — CHALLENGES

Challenges of blockchain

  • Scalability — public blockchains (Bitcoin, Ethereum) process far fewer transactions per second than centralized systems like Visa.
  • High energy consumption — Proof of Work-based blockchains require significant computational power.
  • Regulatory uncertainty — lack of clear global legal frameworks for blockchain and cryptocurrencies.
  • Complexity and skill gap — requires specialized knowledge to develop and maintain.
  • Data immutability drawback — inability to correct erroneous or fraudulent entries once confirmed.
  • Storage issues — ledger size grows continuously, requiring increasing storage capacity.
  • Interoperability — different blockchain platforms often cannot communicate with each other easily.
  • 51% attack risk — if a single entity gains majority control of network computing power, it can manipulate the chain.
  • Privacy concerns — full transparency can conflict with confidentiality needs of businesses and individuals.
BLOCK 06 — APPLICATIONS

Applications of blockchain

Cryptocurrency
Bitcoin, Ethereum, and other digital currencies for peer-to-peer transactions.
Banking & finance
Cross-border payments, smart contracts, fraud reduction.
Supply chain
Tracking goods from origin to destination, ensuring authenticity (Walmart, IBM Food Trust).
Healthcare
Secure sharing of patient records between authorized institutions.
Voting systems
Tamper-proof and transparent electronic voting.
Real estate
Recording property titles and ownership transfers securely.
Identity management
Decentralized digital identities reducing identity theft.
Smart contracts
Self-executing contracts with terms directly written into code (Ethereum-based).
IP & NFTs
Proving ownership and authenticity of digital assets.
Government records
Land registries, licenses, and public records management.
